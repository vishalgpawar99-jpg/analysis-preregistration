# Analysis Preregistration

## 1. Research Question

Does studying for more hours help students get better marks in exams?

## 2. Hypotheses

### Null Hypothesis (H₀)

There is no important relationship between study time and exam marks.

### Alternative Hypothesis (H₁)

There is an important relationship between study time and exam marks.

We expect that students who study more hours may get higher marks.

## 3. Population, Sample, and Exclusions

**Population:**
The population includes first-year college students.

**Sample:**
A selected group of first-year students will be included in the study.

**Unit of Analysis:**
Each record represents one student.

**Inclusion Rules:**
Students who are studying in the first year and provide the required information will be included.

**Exclusion Rules:**
Students with incomplete or invalid information will be excluded.

**Stopping Rule:**
Data collection will stop after reaching the planned sample size.

## 4. Variables and Measures

**Outcome Variable:**
Exam marks will be used as the main outcome variable.

**Predictor Variable:**
Daily study hours will be used as the main predictor.

**Control Variables:**
Other factors such as attendance and previous academic performance may be considered.

**Transformations:**
Data will be changed or transformed only when necessary for statistical analysis.

**Missing Data:**
Records with missing or incomplete information will be handled using a predefined method.

## 5. Analysis Plan

Statistical methods will be selected according to the type of data.

The analysis may include:

* Correlation to check the relationship between study hours and exam marks.
* Linear regression to understand how study hours are related to exam marks.
* t-test or ANOVA if different student groups need to be compared.

The significance level will be **0.05 (5%)**.

Effect sizes will be reported where appropriate. **95% confidence intervals** will also be reported when applicable.

Additional checks will be performed to see whether the main results remain similar under reasonable alternative analyses.

## 6. Deviations

If there are any changes to the original analysis plan, they will be recorded.

Each change will include:

* Date and time
* Original plan
* Change made
* Reason for the change
* Whether the change was made before or after seeing the results

This makes the research process clear, transparent, and easy to understand.
